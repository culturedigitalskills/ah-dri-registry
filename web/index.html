Where can I run my research?

Supercomputers, cloud services, tools and AI assistants open to UK arts and humanities researchers, with who can apply, what it costs and what happens to your data.

Not sure which you need?

Computing services

You bring the code or software; they provide the machine.

Processing power you use to run your own work when your laptop is too slow or too small, for example training or fine-tuning a model, transcribing thousands of hours of audio, or building 3D models from photographs. It comes as CPU or GPU time on university clusters, national supercomputers or cloud platforms. Browser notebooks such as Colab count here too: the interface is theirs, but the work is yours.

Tools and models

You run it.

Software you download or install and operate yourself, on your laptop or on a computing service.

AI services

They run it.

Ready-made AI that someone else hosts and runs: you log in or upload material and get results back. Your material goes to them, which is why this list focuses on what happens to your data.

CPUs are general-purpose processors, good for running many ordinary jobs side by side. GPUs are specialist processors, much faster for AI, images and 3D.

A community registry of UK digital research infrastructure, compiled from public sources. It is maintained by the team at Connecting Arts and Humanities for AI Capability (UKRI7748), funded by UKRI. The wider community helps keep it up to date, and suggestions of new entries or corrections are welcome.
